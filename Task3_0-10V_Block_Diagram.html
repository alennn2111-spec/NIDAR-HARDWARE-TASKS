1. Block diagram

The Blue Pill sends a 12-bit code over I2C. An external DAC turns it into 0–5 V. An op-amp doubles it to 0–10 V. A protection stage sits between the op-amp and the output connector.

2. Function of each block

STM32 Blue Pill

Holds the setpoint and sends the DAC code over I2C. The STM32F103C8 has no on-chip DAC, so an external one is needed.

Its I/O is 3.3 V. The I2C pins used (PB6, PB7) are 5 V-tolerant, but the DAC input threshold needs checking (see section 7).

External DAC

Converts the 12-bit code to a voltage from 0 V up to its supply voltage. Powered from a 5 V rail, full scale is about 5 V.

The supply is also the reference, so any error on the 5 V rail appears directly in the output.

Op-amp conditioning

A non-inverting amplifier with gain 1 + R1/R2 = 2 scales 0–5 V to 0–10 V. It also gives a low-impedance output, so the DAC is not loaded.

It runs from 12 V so its output can swing above 10 V with some headroom.

Output protection

A series resistor isolates the op-amp from cable capacitance and limits fault current. A bidirectional TVS clamps ESD and voltage spikes. An optional small capacitor filters noise.

The series resistor causes a small load-dependent error (section 6).

0–10 V output

A screw terminal (J2) carrying VOUT and signal ground to the receiving device. The load should share the same ground.

7. Open items and verification status

Open design questions

  1. I2C logic levels. A 5 V MCP4725 may need a logic high near 3.5 V. A level shifter is proposed. Confirm the threshold in the datasheet.
  2. Power-up output. Confirm what the DAC outputs at power-up. If it restores a non-zero EEPROM value, the output could be non-zero before firmware runs. Store code 0 in EEPROM, or check the part's default.
  3. 12 V source. The source and its minimum voltage are not decided. For a 10 V output, the op-amp rail (after D1) must stay above 10 V plus the op-amp's output headroom. A battery that sags below about 11 V may not be enough.
  4. Zero end of the range. On a single supply, the op-amp output may not reach exactly 0 V. Check the datasheet and decide if a small offset is acceptable.
  5. Load impedance. Confirm the input impedance of the receiving device, then set R3 and C7.
  6. Short-circuit behaviour. Check how long the op-amp can survive a shorted output.
  7. Resolution and accuracy. 12 bits gives a 2.44 mV step. If this is not enough, a higher-resolution DAC with an external reference is an alternative.
  8. 5 V regulator. Choose a part; check heat dissipation from 12 V.
