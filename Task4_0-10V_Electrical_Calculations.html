1. DAC output range and amplifier gain

The MCP4725 (12-bit) runs from a 5 V rail. Its output goes from 0 V up to the supply voltage.

VDAC,max = 5.000 V × 40954096 = 4.9988 V
Required gain G = 10 V5 V = 2.000
VOUT,max = 2.000 × 4.9988 V = 9.9976 V
Output step = 10 V / 4096 = 2.441 mV

The top code is one step (2.44 mV) below 10 V, which is acceptable. calc

The MCP4725 needs a logic high of at least 0.7 × VDD = 3.5 V. A 3.3 V Blue Pill is below that, so a level shifter is proposed. datasheet

2. Op-amp supply voltage

The op-amp (OPA197) cannot reach its own rail. The supply must be above the largest output by a safe margin.

Largest output (worst case) = 10 V × 1.01 × 1.001 ≈ 10.11 V
Headroom = 0.6 V (conservative)
Minimum op-amp rail = 10.11 + 0.6 = 10.71 V
Minimum input at J1 = 10.71 + 0.5 V (diode D1) = 11.21 V

Proposed: a regulated 12 V supply, within ±5 %. The OPA197 works from 4.5 V to 36 V, so 12 V is inside its range. If the supply can drop below about 11.2 V (for example a battery), use 15 V instead. assumed datasheet

Assumes: 5 V rail ±1 %, resistors ±0.1 %, D1 drop 0.5 V, headroom 0.6 V.

3. Feedback resistor values

G = 1 + RfRg = 2  ⇒  Rf = Rg
Choose Rf = Rg = 10 kΩ
Feedback current = 10 V20 kΩ = 0.5 mA

10 kΩ keeps the load on the op-amp small (1 kΩ would draw 5 mA) and keeps errors from bias current and noise negligible. calc

4. Output voltage limits and saturation

The output follows the DAC only while it stays inside the op-amp's range. Outside it, the output flattens and the feedback no longer controls it.

Upper limit = op-amp rail − 0.6 V
12 V input: rail ≈ 11.5 V → limit ≈ 10.9 V (OK for 10 V)
10.8 V input (−10 %): rail ≈ 10.3 V → limit ≈ 9.7 V (clips above code ≈ 3973)
  • Top end: if the supply is too low, the highest codes all give the same voltage.
  • Bottom end: on a single supply the output cannot go below 0 V. It may sit a few millivolts above 0 V at code 0 (TI quotes up to about 25 mV from the rail with no load, for the positive rail).
  • Power-up: the MCP4725 factory default is mid-scale (code 2048), so the output would be about 5 V at power-up until firmware writes code 0.

5. Output current and load resistance limits

A 100 Ω series resistor (R3) protects the output. It also lowers the voltage when the load is small.

  • Recommended load: 10 kΩ or higher (about 1 mA, error about 1 %).
  • Planning limit: keep op-amp output current under 10 mA, which means a load of about 1 kΩ or more. assumed
  • The OPA197 short-circuit current is about ±65 mA. This is not a continuous rating. datasheet
  • Shorted output: the op-amp limits at 65 mA, so R3 dissipates about 0.42 W and the op-amp about 0.33 W. R3 should be rated for this (1 W or more). calc

The 5 V regulator is the main heat source. The 70 mA load is an estimate. calc assumed

Safety points

  • R3: choose its power rating for a shorted output (0.42 W), not for normal use.
  • TVS diode (SMAJ12CA): 12 V stand-off, 13.3–14.7 V breakdown, 19.9 V maximum clamp. It does not conduct at 10 V. It protects against short transients only, not a continuous overvoltage such as a wiring mistake. datasheet
  • MCP4725 supply: it is a 5 V part. If the 5 V regulator fails, 11.5 V could reach the DAC and the Blue Pill.
  • Capacitors: use at least 25 V parts on the 12 V rail. assumed
